Your budget is tight. You posted on Upwork. Within an hour, you've got 20 proposals.

One stands out: a developer in India, `$8/hr`, 5 stars, "Expert in iOS shipping." The portfolio looks decent. You do the math: `$3,200` for what a US developer would charge `$15,000` for.

You think: "This could work."

I'm going to show you exactly why it usually doesn't.

## The Real Issues With Offshore

I'm not saying offshore developers are bad. Some are excellent. I'm saying offshore developers struggle specifically with app shipping, and here's why.

### Timezone Hell

You're in California. Your developer is in India. That's a `13–14` hour difference.

You wake up at 9am. You have a question: "Apple rejected for guideline 4.2. Can you review the crash logs and let me know what's happening?"

He's asleep. Won't see your message for 12+ hours.

By the time he responds at 11pm your time, you've already gone to bed. You see the response in the morning. His response raises 3 new questions. You ask those questions at 9am. He sees them at 11pm his time. Responds at midnight your time. You see it the next morning.

A conversation that should take 30 minutes takes 3 days.

And here's the thing: app shipping has a lot of back-and-forth. You need clarity on what Apple's rejection actually means. You need to verify the fix before it's resubmitted. You need to coordinate timing around Apple's review queue.

Each round trip through 13+ hours of timezone delay costs you a day.

Real example: "Apple rejected on day 3. Wanted to resubmit by day 5. Took until day 11 because of timezone delays. We missed our launch window."

### Communication Gaps

App store submissions live in the details.

Apple's guidelines are written in legal language. When Apple says "minimum functionality," it means something specific. When they say "business model clarity," they mean something else. The developers who understand this are the ones who've read these guidelines carefully and fought with Apple before.

An offshore developer working through a language barrier? They read the rejection and think, "This doesn't make sense." They don't know the context. They don't know that Apple has a specific definition of "minimal functionality" or that "business model clarity" is Apple's way of saying "we don't understand how you make money."

So they resubmit without addressing the core issue. Apple rejects again. You're frustrated. They're frustrated. And you're back in the timezone delay loop.

**Real example:** "App rejected for 3.1.1 (business model not clear). Offshore dev didn't understand what Apple meant. Resubmitted the same app. Rejected again. We had to hire a US-based person to appeal it with a detailed explanation of the business model. Cost: `$800` to fix something that should've been fixed in the first resubmit."

### No Accountability During Crises

It's 2am your time. You're checking your email. Your app just got rejected with a critical issue. You need to talk to your developer urgently.

Your offshore developer is asleep. They won't be awake for 8 more hours. You can't call them. You can't get real-time help. You're stuck.

And here's where it gets expensive: when there's a crisis, you either:

1. Wait 8+ hours for them to wake up (losing momentum)
2. Hire someone else to fix it immediately (expensive)
3. Do it yourself (you're not trained)

I had a founder tell me: "Hired an offshore dev. App got rejected with a critical payment issue. He was asleep when I found out. By the time he woke up, I'd hired someone else to fix it. Total cost for a 2-hour fix: `$1,200` because of timing."

### Compliance and Legal Context

App shipping in the US isn't just technical. It's regulatory.

Apple requires privacy policies that comply with CCPA, GDPR, and other frameworks. They require specific language around data collection. They require proof of compliance for financial apps. They care about age ratings for content.

A developer in India might not understand these nuances. They might never have built an app that had to comply with US/EU data laws. They submit with a privacy policy they think is fine, and Apple rejects because it doesn't meet CCPA standards.

Now you need to hire a legal reviewer, or you need to find someone who understands US data privacy. Another `$500–$2,000` added to the cost.

### The Math Gets Ugly Fast

Let's walk through a realistic scenario:

You hire an offshore developer for `$8/hr`. You think it'll take 80 hours. You budget `$640`.

What actually happens:

- **Initial build:** 80 hours * `$8` = `$640`
- **First submission:** App gets rejected for something the developer misunderstood
- **Back-and-forth rounds:** 3 rounds of timezone-delayed feedback = 15 hours of work that feels like 30 hours in calendar time
- **Actual rework:** 20 hours * `$8` = `$160`
- **Hiring someone to appeal:** The offshore dev can't handle the Apple rejection properly, so you hire a US-based person for `$500` to write a proper appeal
- **Total cost:** `$640 + $160 + $500 = $1,300`
- **Total time:** 8–10 weeks

Compare that to hiring a US-based developer:

- **Cost:** `$50/hr * 100 hours = $5,000`
- **Time:** 3–4 weeks
- **Rejections:** Handled the first time

You saved `$3,700` upfront and lost it in delays and emergency hiring. Plus you burned 2 months of time.

## When Offshore CAN Work

There are a few cases where offshore developers work fine for app shipping:

### 1. Coding Only (You Handle Submission)

Hire them to write the code. You handle the App Store submission yourself. This shifts the responsibility to you, but it means timezone delays don't matter as much. You're not waiting for real-time feedback. You can test everything, catch issues, and then submit cleanly.

**Cost:** Reasonable
**Timeline:** Longer (because you're learning submission)
**Risk:** High (you're doing something you're not trained for)

This works if you have time and you're willing to spend 40–60 hours learning the submission process.

### 2. Non-Critical Features

If you hire an offshore dev to build non-essential features—UI refinements, secondary flows, etc.—timezone delays matter less. Rejections are unlikely. The work is straightforward.

You keep shipping and core functionality with someone US-based. You give secondary work to offshore.

**Cost:** Low
**Timeline:** Moderate
**Risk:** Low

This works if you have a hybrid team.

### 3. They've Actually Shipped US App Store Apps

Rare, but some offshore developers have real experience shipping to the App Store. They understand Apple's process. They've been rejected before. They know the guidelines.

If you find this person, the timezone problem is still real, but at least they have the knowledge.

**Cost:** Higher than typical offshore
**Timeline:** Still slow
**Risk:** Moderate (you still have timezone delays, but they're more competent)

To find them, ask: "How many apps have you shipped to the iOS App Store specifically?" The ones who say "5+" might actually know what they're doing.

### 4. You Have Bandwidth to Manage Them Daily

This is important: **Managing an offshore developer takes work.**

Daily standups (across timezones). Detailed specs (they'll ask questions you didn't anticipate). Code reviews (you need to catch mistakes before submission). Build testing (they might not test on all devices).

If you have 5–10 hours per week to manage them closely, offshore can work. If you're hiring them to be independent, it falls apart fast.

## The Real Decision Tree

Here's how to think about it:

**Do you have time?** If no, don't go offshore. Time zone delays will destroy you. Use a flat-fee service instead.

**Do you have a real deadline?** If yes, don't go offshore. You can't afford 8–10 week timelines with rework cycles.

**Is this a side project?** If yes, offshore coding + your own submission could work.

**Is this your main product?** If yes, hire US-based or use a service.

**Can you verify and manage offshore work daily?** If no, don't do it. If yes, maybe it works for coding-only.

The offshore developer isn't the problem. The problem is that app shipping isn't a solo remote job. It requires communication, context, and timezone overlap. Offshore works great for async work. App shipping is synchronous.
