This framework systematically maps search intents and content gaps to maximize Google traction.

Instead of just pillar categories, think about **search intent**: what is someone actually looking for when they search?

## Content Quality Standards

**Every post must include:**
1. **Personal experience** — You've done this. Show it.
2. **Real examples** — Specific tools, specific errors, real screenshots
3. **Actual timelines** — "3-5 days" not "some time"

5. **Links to related content** — 2-3 internal links minimum
6. **Actionable next step** — What should they do next? → Link to `/kickoff` or related post

**What NOT to include:**
- Invented statistics ("70% of developers...")
- Fluffy adjectives ("Amazing", "incredible")
- Excessive warnings or hedging
- Outdated screenshots
- Generic advice (don't just repeat what's already on the web)

**Writing voice:** Keep your direct, tactical voice. Your readers are tired of marketing fluff. They want:
- Straight talk (no corporate language)
- Specific numbers (not "affordable" → "$299/mo")
- Proof (not promises)
- Speed (they're busy shipping)

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

1. **Writing about everything once** — You'll dilute your authority. Own 3-5 topics deeply.
2. **Publishing then abandoning** — Old posts with 2019 screenshots look neglected. Refresh quarterly.
3. **No internal linking** — Orphaned posts don't help SEO. Link everything.
4. **Copying competitors** — Google rewards original research and voice. Use your experience.
5. **Not including CTAs** — Blog traffic is worthless if it doesn't convert. Link to `/kickoff` naturally.
6. **Ignoring search intent** — Write for the search, not for what you want to say.
7. **Too short/fluffy** — Google now favors depth. 2000+ words standard for competitive queries.
8. **Infrequent publishing** — 1 post/month gets buried. 2/week shows you're an authority.
